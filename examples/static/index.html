scrollbar

A thin bar of your own along the right edge. Point at the edge or scroll, and it shows; drag it, or press the strip to glide there. It fades 0.6s after scrolling stops.

The browser's own bar is hidden from the first frame — by CSS, not the script — so nothing shifts sideways when the script arrives. On a touch screen, or with no script, the browser's bar stays.

The page's gutter — what is centred, and what is hidden

The bar floats over the page, so what you can see ends where its strip begins, 18px from the right. The dashed line marks the middle of what you can see. Point at the right edge to bring the bar up.

With data-pg-scrollbar-gutter the row's content is laid out in what the bar leaves: the box sits on the line, the tag at the end stays in sight. Without it the row is laid out in the whole width: the box is half a strip to the right of the line, the tag is under the bar. Give it to the content sections; leave it off a full-width slider, a banner, a video.

A panel's bar — without the gutter, and with it

Each list scrolls; its bar sits beside it in the panel's box and names it: data-pg-scrollbar="#plain". The list's own bar is hidden in every browser, Firefox too. Scroll either list, or point at its right edge, and watch the numbers.

A pop-up

The same bar inside a pop-up. While it is open the page is locked, and the page's bar steps away — it sees the page cannot scroll.
